# Article – How to write a good English composition (about 400 words)

How to Write a Good English Composition

Many students believe that good English writing depends on knowing thousands of difficult words. In fact, the best compositions I have read were written by students who planned carefully, organised their ideas clearly and used simple language correctly. Here is how you can do the same.

Start by reading the question twice. Underline the text type, the audience and the task. A letter to your principal and a blog post for your classmates may cover the same topic, but they require completely different tones. If the question lists points you must include, number them and make sure each one appears in your composition. Missing a required point is the most common and most costly mistake in examinations.

Next, spend five minutes planning. Write down your main idea, then list three supporting points, each with a concrete example. A plan does not need to be beautiful. A few words in the margin are enough to stop you from wandering off topic halfway through, which is what happens when students begin writing immediately.

When you write, give each paragraph one job. The opening paragraph should explain why you are writing and state your position. Each middle paragraph should begin with a topic sentence, develop one point with an example and end by showing why that point matters. The closing paragraph should bring your ideas together and leave the reader with a clear message, a call to action or a thought to remember. Linking words such as however, in addition and as a result guide the reader from one idea to the next, but use them naturally rather than in every sentence.

Choose accuracy over decoration. A short, correct sentence earns more marks than a long, impressive one with a grammar mistake in the middle. Vary your sentence length so that the writing has rhythm, and keep your tenses consistent. Stories usually stay in the past tense, while letters and articles usually stay in the present.

Finally, save the last five minutes for checking. Look for subject-verb agreement, missing articles, plural endings and spelling. These small errors are easy to fix and they add up quickly.

Writing well is a skill, not a talent. Read widely, write regularly, learn from your corrections and follow these steps every time. Within a few months, you will notice that the words come more easily and the marks follow.

By Chris Wong, 5B
