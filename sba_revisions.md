## English SBA – Apollo 13 (revised, about 480 words, roughly 4 minutes)

Good afternoon, everyone. Today I would like to share a book I read recently, Apollo 13. It is the true story of three astronauts, Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert and Fred Haise, who survived a devastating explosion on their spacecraft in 1970, more than 200,000 miles from Earth. The book is not only an account of their ordeal in space. It also shows how perseverance, intelligence, teamwork and even luck combined to bring them home safely.

Let me first explain what happened. Two days into the mission, an oxygen tank exploded, destroying one of the three fuel cells and sending oxygen jetting into space. With only about fifteen minutes of power left in the command module, the crew moved into the lunar module, which became their lifeboat. What inspired me most was the response on the ground. NASA engineers, together with Ken Mattingly, an astronaut who had been removed from the crew days earlier because of exposure to German measles, worked around the clock to invent solutions for each new problem, such as building a carbon dioxide filter out of spare parts. The mission failed to reach the Moon, yet the rescue became one of NASA's greatest triumphs.

I learned three lessons from this book. The first is that we are far more capable than we believe. Failure is easy, but learning from failure is hard. The astronauts and engineers worked by trial and error, eliminating what did not work until they found the sequence that would bring the crew home. We learn more from what fails than from what succeeds.

The second lesson is about hope. When the crew finally saw the command module with one side blown away right up to the heat shield, they knew they might burn up on re-entry even if everything else went right. Still, they stayed calm and kept working. I had never realised how much hope matters until I read this book. Without it, there is no reason to keep going when the odds are against us.

The third lesson is emotional control. At one point, two of the astronauts began arguing, and Commander Lovell stopped them at once, reminding them that fighting would change nothing. I sometimes struggle to control my own emotions, so this scene struck me deeply. Arguing does not solve problems. It only makes teamwork harder.

I believe these lessons apply directly to us as we prepare for the DSE. None of us should lose hope, lose our temper or slip into a negative mindset, because these are the tools that keep us on course. The book also reminded me that success is not only about working hard alone. It depends on the people around us. I now try to forgive quickly and avoid holding grudges, because the relationships I damage today may be exactly the ones I need tomorrow.

Thank you for listening.

## 中文 SBA – 一隻雁讀通三個單元（書面語修訂版，約 850 字）

現代人求婚送戒指，三千年前的古人求婚送甚麼？答案是一隻雁。書中記載，古人結婚要行「六禮」，其中五禮都要用雁。為甚麼不送金銀，偏偏送一隻鳥？這個問題引導我讀通了三個單元：社會、藝術和學術思想。我發現，中國文化講求的是「恰到好處」：看重的不只是物質，而是心意；不是填滿，而是留白；不是絕對，而是因時制宜。

社會單元中，我印象最深刻的是婚姻禮俗。雁是候鳥，秋去春回，從不失約；而且雁一生只配一偶，失偶不再另配。書中引《易‧序卦》說，夫婦之道貴乎恆久，雁正是堅貞與專一的象徵，呼應了一夫一妻制的精神。六禮之中，只有納徵一禮送財物，其餘五禮都用雁，可見古人把婚姻的哲學寄託在這隻鳥身上。這令我明白，在婚姻裏，堅貞比物質更能令關係長久。我反思到，今天的戒指雖然圓，象徵生活圓滿，但圓滿只是結果，長久相伴、一心一意，才是通往圓滿的道路。

藝術單元和學術思想單元也有類似的智慧。孔子提出「中庸」，中庸不是平庸，而是做事恰到好處；他又說「克己復禮為仁」，提醒我們「禮」的形式要以「仁」的心意為本，不能死板。南宋畫家馬遠，人稱「馬一角」，他的畫往往只集中於一角，其餘留白，書中形容這「予人遐想的餘地」。過去我以為畫得越多越好，現在才明白，留白反而能讓觀者想像更多。莊子也說，大木材可以用來建屋，卻堵不住一個小洞，可見價值不是絕對的，而是視乎處境。

這部分對我這個高中生很有啟發。我們的生活很緊張：上課、補習、興趣班，連空閒時間也被手機佔據；同學之間為了成績和獎項互相競爭，越鬥越累。但有時放空一下，做些看似無用但自己喜歡的事，反而令我們安靜下來，減少焦慮，對學業也有幫助。在這種情況下，留白比填滿更有用。

總括而言，三個單元內容雖然不同，背後的道理卻是相通的。一隻雁教我看見心意比物質重要，一幅畫教我看見留白比填滿可貴，一句「中庸」教我看見恰到好處比極端更有智慧。這三樣東西，正是我在緊張的中學生活中最需要學會的。
