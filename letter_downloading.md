# Letter to the editor of the Young Post – Downloading entertainment from the internet (about 400 words)

Dear Editor,

I am writing in response to the recent conviction of a man who uploaded movies to the internet and allowed thousands of strangers to download them. As a computer enthusiast who has spent many evenings downloading music and films, I followed the case with a mixture of interest and discomfort, because it forced me to think honestly about something I had always preferred not to question.

Let me begin with the rights, as the downloaders see them. The internet was built for sharing, and for teenagers with limited pocket money, a film that costs a hundred dollars in the cinema and nothing online is a tempting choice. Many of us also argue that we download songs we would never have bought anyway, so nobody actually loses a sale. Some even claim that sharing helps unknown artists find an audience.

The wrongs, however, are harder to deny once you look closely. Every film is the work of hundreds of people, from actors to the technicians who paint the sets, and all of them are paid from ticket sales and legal downloads. When we take their work for free, we are not sharing. We are helping ourselves to something that belongs to someone else, and the fact that it is easy does not make it right. I would be furious if someone copied a program I had written and gave it away. It is dishonest of me to expect musicians to feel differently.

Will the conviction stop people? I doubt it will stop everyone. The man was caught because he uploaded films on a large scale, and most young people assume that a single download is too small to be noticed. Many of my friends have already shrugged and carried on. Human nature being what it is, people break speed limits every day even though the cameras are clearly visible.

Yet I believe the case will change behaviour over time, for two reasons. First, the risk is now real rather than theoretical. Once a few more ordinary users are prosecuted, the calculation will shift. Second, and more importantly, legal services have become cheap and convenient. When a monthly subscription costs less than a single cinema ticket, breaking the law begins to look not only risky but pointless.

As for me, I have cancelled my illegal downloads and signed up for a streaming service. It cost me less than I expected, and my conscience is much lighter.

Yours faithfully,
Chris Wong
