# Speech for the school assembly – Caring for senior citizens

Good morning, Principal Chan, teachers and fellow students.

Three weeks ago, I spent a Saturday afternoon knocking on doors in a public housing estate in Sham Shui Po. I was one of twenty volunteers from our school's Community Service Club visiting elderly residents who live alone. I expected to deliver some rice and leave. Instead, I came home with a lesson I want to share with you today.

The first flat we visited belonged to Mrs Lam, a ninety-year-old widow. Her home was tidy, but her fridge held only a few eggs, and her only chair faced a television that no longer worked. When we asked what she needed, she did not mention food or money. She simply said, "Nobody has talked to me since Tuesday." That moment taught me what senior citizens truly need. Yes, many of them need practical help, such as hot meals, repairs to leaking taps, and someone to read the small print on their medicine bottles. But above all, they need company, respect and the feeling that they still matter to someone.

So what can we, as students, do? More than you might think. We can join the monthly visits that our club organises, where an hour of chatting or playing cards brightens someone's whole week. We can teach the elderly in our own buildings to make video calls, so that they can see their grandchildren's faces. We can offer to carry groceries up the stairs, or simply greet the old man who sits alone in the park every morning. None of these actions costs money. They only cost a little time, and a little courage to start the conversation.

Some of you may be wondering why this is our responsibility. Let me give you three reasons. First, these are the people who built the Hong Kong we enjoy today. Mrs Lam worked in a garment factory for forty years so that her children could go to school. We owe them our gratitude. Second, Hong Kong is ageing rapidly, and by the time we are adults, one in three people will be over sixty-five. If we do not build a caring culture now, we will inherit a lonely city. Third, and most honestly, caring for others changes us. I walked into that estate thinking I was giving my time away. I walked out having gained patience, perspective and a friend.

Mrs Lam asked us to visit again. I promised her we would, and I would like some of you to come with me. Sign-up sheets are outside the library, and our next visit is on the fifteenth of this month.

Thank you for listening, and I hope to see you there.
