# Article for a local business magazine – Making the workplace environmentally friendly

Going Green at Work: Small Changes, Big Returns

By Chris Wong, Green Office Team, TechWave Solutions

Six months ago, our IT company's office in Kwun Tong looked like most others in Hong Kong. Lights blazed all day, the air conditioning was set to a chilly eighteen degrees and bins overflowed with paper cups. Then our managing director challenged a small team, which I am part of, to make the office environmentally friendly within a year. We are halfway through, and what we have learned may surprise owners who believe going green is expensive or complicated.

The easiest place to start is energy. Lighting and air conditioning make up most of a typical Hong Kong office's electricity bill. We replaced our old tubes with LED panels, installed motion sensors in meeting rooms and corridors, and raised the thermostat to twenty-four degrees, the level recommended by the Environmental Protection Department. We also set every computer to sleep after fifteen idle minutes. These changes cost less than one month's electricity bill, and our power consumption has already fallen by nearly a quarter.

Paper and waste come next. As an IT company, we had little excuse for printing, yet we were using thousands of sheets a month. Digital signatures, double-sided printing by default and clearly labelled recycling bins beside every printer cut our paper use in half. We replaced disposable cups with ceramic mugs and installed a water filter so that nobody needs bottled water. The pantry now produces a fraction of the rubbish it once did.

A third area, often overlooked, is how staff travel and what they eat. We introduced flexible hours so that employees can avoid rush-hour traffic, allowed two days of working from home each week and arranged a discount with a nearby vegetarian café. Fewer car journeys and less meat mean a smaller carbon footprint without any new equipment.

Why should every business consider following this path? The first reason is money. Lower electricity, paper and water bills go straight to the bottom line, and the savings continue year after year. The second is talent. Younger employees increasingly want to work for companies whose values match their own, and our green programme has become a genuine selling point in interviews. The third is reputation. Clients now ask about sustainability in tenders, and a company that can show real action has an advantage. Finally, there is simple responsibility. Hong Kong's landfills are almost full and our summers are growing hotter. Businesses, which consume most of the city's electricity, cannot leave the problem to households alone.

Going green has not slowed our company down. If anything, it has given our staff a shared goal and made the office a more pleasant place. If a team of five people in an IT firm can transform a workplace in six months, any business can.
